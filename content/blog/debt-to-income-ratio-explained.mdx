Debt-to-income ratio. It sounds like something out of a finance textbook. But it is one of the most important numbers in your home buying journey, and it is easier to understand than most people expect.

I have had plenty of clients in Las Vegas assume their DTI was a dealbreaker. Then we run the actual numbers and it turns out they are totally fine. So let's break it down together, no jargon.

## What DTI Actually Is

Your debt-to-income ratio is a simple comparison. It looks at how much of your monthly income goes toward debt payments.

Here is the plain version. Take your monthly debt payments. Divide them by your monthly income before taxes. That gives you a percentage. That percentage is your DTI.

Lenders use it to answer one question. Can you comfortably handle a mortgage payment on top of what you already owe? That is it. It is a comfort check, and comfort is exactly how I think about your loan too.

## What Counts and What Does Not

This trips people up all the time, so let's be clear. Not every bill you pay counts toward DTI.

Things that usually count:

- Car loans and car leases.
- Credit card minimum payments.
- Student loans.
- Personal loans.
- Any other loan that shows on your credit report.
- The new mortgage payment you are applying for.

Things that usually do not count:

- Groceries.
- Utilities like power and water.
- Cell phone bills.
- Streaming subscriptions.
- Gas and everyday spending.

See the difference? DTI is not about your whole budget. It is about debt that shows up on your credit report. A lot of the stuff that stresses your wallet does not even factor in. That is why so many folks find out their number is better than they feared.

## Why Yours Might Be Just Fine

Here is the good news. Lenders allow more room than most people assume.

Depending on the loan program, you can often carry a DTI that feels higher than you would guess. And because I work with multiple wholesale lenders, I can shop your file to the program that gives you the most breathing room. One lender might say no. Another says yes to the same exact numbers.

So if you have a car payment and a couple of cards, do not count yourself out. That is normal. Most buyers in Henderson and Las Vegas have some debt. The question is not "do you have debt." The question is whether the payment fits.

## How to Improve Your DTI Before You Apply

If your number is a little high, there are ways to bring it down. Some are quick. Some take a little planning.

- Pay off a small loan. If you have a card or loan with a low balance, knocking it out removes that whole monthly payment from the math.
- Avoid new debt. Do not finance a new car or open a new card right before you buy. A new payment raises your DTI at the worst time.
- Boost documented income. A raise, a bonus, or side income you can prove can all help the ratio.
- Choose a payment that fits. Since the new mortgage counts in your DTI, picking a comfortable payment keeps the whole ratio healthy. This is the payment-first approach, and it works.

You do not need to eliminate all your debt. You just need the right balance between what you owe and what you earn.
