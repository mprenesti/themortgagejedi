## What a Bank Statement Loan Is

A bank statement loan lets you qualify for a mortgage using your bank deposits instead of your tax returns. The lender reviews your recent statements, usually the last 12 to 24 months, and uses your deposits to figure out your income.

This is a Non-QM loan, which means it does not follow the strict standard rulebook. That flexibility is the whole point. It lets us show a lender who you really are as an earner, not just the number that survives after your accountant works their magic.

The result is a real mortgage, on a real home, based on real income. It just measures that income in a way that fits how business owners actually operate.

## Who These Loans Are For

Bank statement loans are made for people whose tax returns do not reflect their true earning power. Around Las Vegas and Henderson, that is a lot of hardworking people:

- Business owners of all kinds, from restaurants to auto shops
- Real estate agents and loan pros paid on commission
- Contractors, landscapers, and trades running their own crews
- Consultants, freelancers, and creatives
- Salon, spa, and gym owners
- Anyone with strong deposits but heavy tax write offs

If you have been self-employed for at least two years and your deposits are steady, you are likely a strong candidate.

## How the Income Math Works

Here is roughly how a lender turns your statements into a qualifying income:

- They add up your business deposits over the review period
- They apply an expense factor, since not every dollar of revenue is income
- The remaining amount becomes your qualifying income

That expense factor varies. Some lenders use a set percentage, others adjust based on your type of business. This is one of the biggest reasons the right lender matters so much, because a better expense factor means more qualifying income for you.

Personal account statements can sometimes be used too, depending on how you run your finances. We look at your setup and choose the approach that shows your income in the best honest light.

## What You Will Need

Every lender is a little different, but here is the general checklist:

- 12 to 24 months of bank statements, business or personal
- Proof you are self-employed, like a business license
- A credit score that shows you handle your obligations
- A down payment, usually a bit larger than a standard loan
- Some reserves to show you can cover a few payments

The rate on a bank statement loan usually runs a little higher than a conventional loan. That is the trade for skipping the tax returns and getting flexible underwriting. When we run your numbers, we make sure that rate still leaves you with a comfortable payment.
