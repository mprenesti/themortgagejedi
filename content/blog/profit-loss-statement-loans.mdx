If you run your own business, you already know the drill. You work hard, you make good money, and then your accountant does everything legal to lower your taxable income. That is smart tax planning. The problem shows up when you try to buy a home and a traditional lender looks at your tax returns and sees a number that feels way too low.

I hear this all the time from self-employed folks here in Las Vegas. You feel like you are being punished for being good at business. A profit and loss loan, or P&L loan, is built for exactly this situation. Let me walk you through it.

## What a P&L Loan Actually Is

A P&L loan lets you qualify based on a profit and loss statement instead of your tax returns. That statement shows your business revenue and expenses over a set period, usually the last 12 to 24 months. The lender uses the bottom line from that statement to figure out your income.

Here is the key part. You are not stuck with the tiny net income that shows up after every write off on your tax return. The P&L reflects the actual money your business is producing before all the tax strategy kicks in.

This is a Non-QM loan, which means it does not follow the standard government backed rulebook. That is a good thing for business owners. It gives us more room to show your real financial picture.

## Who These Loans Are For

P&L loans work best for people who have a real, steady business but a messy or misleading tax return. That covers a lot of Las Vegas and Henderson folks. Think about:

- Restaurant and bar owners on the Strip and off it
- Real estate agents and brokers
- Contractors and trades running their own crews
- Salon and med spa owners
- Rideshare and delivery business owners who have grown past a side gig
- Consultants and freelancers with strong cash flow

If your business has been running for at least two years and you can show consistent income, you are probably a good fit.

## What You Need to Qualify

Every lender is a little different, but here is what usually comes into play:

- A profit and loss statement, often prepared or signed off by a CPA or tax preparer
- Proof that your business is real and active, like a business license or a few months of bank statements
- A credit score that shows you handle your obligations
- A down payment, which tends to be a bit higher than a standard loan
- Enough reserves to show you can handle a few payments if things get slow

The rate on a P&L loan is usually a little higher than a conventional loan. That is the trade off for the flexibility. But do not let that scare you off before we run the numbers.
