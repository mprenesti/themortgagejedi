A lot of people in Las Vegas think they need 20 percent down to buy a home. That is one of the biggest myths out there. The truth is there are real programs in Nevada built to help you get in the door with far less, and some of them help with your down payment and closing costs too.

But here is the part most people skip. Down payment help is not free money that lands in your lap with no strings. Each program has rules, and some of them change your monthly payment in ways you need to understand before you sign anything. My job is to make sure the help you take actually leaves you with a payment you are comfortable with. Not just the biggest number a lender will approve.

Let me walk you through what is actually out there.

## The Nevada Housing Division Programs

The state of Nevada runs assistance through the Nevada Housing Division. The best known option is Home Is Possible. It can give you money toward your down payment and closing costs, usually as a percentage of your loan amount.

A few things to know:

- It is often structured as a second loan or a grant depending on the version you pick.
- There are income limits and a home price cap, and those get updated over time.
- You usually need to take a short homebuyer education course. It is not hard and it actually teaches you useful stuff.

The tradeoff is simple. You put less cash in up front, but the assistance can nudge your interest rate or your payment a little higher. Sometimes that tradeoff is worth it. Sometimes keeping more cash in your pocket for repairs and moving costs is the smarter move. We look at your real numbers and decide together.

## FHA Loans and Low Down Payment Options

Down payment assistance often pairs with an FHA loan. FHA lets you buy with as little as 3.5 percent down, and the credit requirements are friendlier than a lot of people expect.

There are also conventional loan options that allow 3 percent down for first-time buyers. Which one fits you depends on your credit, your income, and how long you plan to stay in the home. A conventional loan with a bit more down can sometimes beat FHA on the monthly payment once you factor in mortgage insurance. This is exactly the kind of thing I run the math on for you.

## What About Grants From Cities and Counties

Depending on where you are looking, Henderson and other parts of the valley sometimes have local programs tied to specific price points or neighborhoods. These come and go based on funding, so what was available last year may be closed this year.

Here is my honest advice. Do not fall in love with a specific grant before you know it is open and funded. Programs run out of money. Instead, get pre-approved first, then we match you to whatever help is actually live at the moment you are ready to buy.

## The Payment-First Way to Look at Assistance

This is where I am different from a lot of loan officers. I do not start with the biggest loan you can get. I start with the payment you can live with.

When we look at any assistance program, I ask a few questions:

- What does this do to your total monthly payment, including taxes and insurance?
- Does the help come with a payment you still feel good about two years from now?
- Are you keeping enough cash in reserve so a broken water heater does not wreck your month?

If a program gets you in the door but leaves you stressed every first of the month, that is not a win. A comfortable payment is the whole point of owning a home in Las Vegas instead of renting one.
