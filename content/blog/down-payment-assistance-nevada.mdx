The down payment is the single biggest barrier keeping renters out of homeownership, and most buyers in this market have no idea how much help is actually available to them right here in Nevada.

## Nevada Housing Division Home Is Possible Program

This is one of the most widely used programs for Nevada buyers. It offers down payment assistance in the form of a grant or a low-interest second loan, depending on the specific program option chosen, that can cover a meaningful portion of your down payment and closing costs. Some versions of this assistance never have to be repaid at all, as long as you meet occupancy requirements and stay in the home for a defined period.

## Program Structure Options

Depending on which version fits your situation, assistance may come as a true grant with no repayment obligation, a deferred second loan with no monthly payment that becomes due upon sale or refinance, or a low-interest second loan repaid alongside your primary mortgage. The right structure depends on your first mortgage type and your long-term plans for the home.

## Who Typically Qualifies

Income limits apply and vary by household size and county, since Clark County limits differ from more rural parts of the state. Most programs require the home to be your primary residence, not an investment property. Credit score minimums typically align closely with the minimum required by your first mortgage program, often in the 640 to 660 range depending on the specific assistance option.

## First-Time Buyer Is Not Always Required

A common myth is that down payment assistance is exclusively for first-time buyers. Many Nevada programs actually define first-time buyer as someone who has not owned a home in the past three years, which opens the door to buyers who owned previously but have been renting for a while.

## How These Programs Actually Get Used

Down payment assistance is typically layered on top of an FHA, VA, USDA, or conventional first mortgage. It is not a standalone loan. Your first mortgage still needs to meet its own qualifying guidelines, the assistance simply reduces or eliminates what you need to bring to closing for the down payment and sometimes closing costs.

## The Catch Most Buyers Don't Expect

Assistance programs can come with occupancy requirements, meaning you generally need to live in the home as your primary residence for a set number of years, or a portion of the assistance may need to be repaid if you sell or refinance early. This is not a hidden trap, it is standard structure for these programs, but it is worth understanding fully before you commit.

## Why So Few Buyers Use This Money

Most buyers assume these programs are either a myth, too complicated, or reserved for people in much lower income brackets than they are. In reality, income limits in Clark County are often higher than people assume, and the application process, while it does require documentation, is not dramatically different from a standard mortgage application.
