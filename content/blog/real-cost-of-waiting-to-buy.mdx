I hear it every week. "Mike, I think I am going to wait." People want to wait for lower rates, or lower prices, or just the perfect moment. I understand the instinct. Buying a home is a big deal.

But waiting is not free. It often costs more than people realize. So let us do something simple. Let us run the actual math and see what waiting really does to your budget in Las Vegas.

## What People Are Usually Waiting For

Most folks who wait are hoping for one of two things.

- Home prices will drop.
- Interest rates will drop.

Both feel smart. But here is the catch. These two things do not always move the way we hope, and they often work against each other. When rates drop, more buyers jump in and prices tend to rise. When prices soften, rates are often higher. It is a seesaw.

Trying to time both perfectly is like trying to catch two falling leaves at once. Possible, but rare.

## The Rent Math

Start with the most obvious cost. Rent.

Say you pay two thousand dollars a month in rent while you wait. Wait one year and that is twenty four thousand dollars gone. Not toward equity. Not toward anything you own. It is gone.

Wait two years and you are near fifty thousand dollars in rent paid. That is money that could have been building your own wealth instead of your landlord's.

## The Price Math

Now add home prices. Real estate in the Las Vegas and Henderson area has trended upward over time. Let us use a simple, modest example.

Say a home costs four hundred thousand dollars today. If prices rise just 4 percent over the next year, that same home costs about four hundred sixteen thousand next year. That is sixteen thousand dollars more for the same house.

So while you waited to save money, the price moved away from you. You did not just pay rent. You also chase a higher price.

## The Payment Math

Here is the piece I care about most, because I am payment-first. Let us look at how rate changes hit your monthly payment.

On a four hundred thousand dollar loan, a rate that is even 0.5 percent higher can add roughly one hundred to one hundred fifty dollars to your monthly payment. A full 1 percent higher can add a few hundred dollars a month.

Now stretch that over 30 years. A payment that is two hundred dollars higher every month is over seventy thousand dollars across the life of the loan. Waiting for a better rate can backfire if rates rise instead of fall.

## Marry the House, Date the Rate

Here is a phrase I love. Marry the house, date the rate.

The idea is simple. If you find a home that fits your life and your budget today, buying it locks in the price. If rates drop later, you can refinance into a lower payment. You are not stuck.

But if you wait and the price climbs, you cannot go back and buy at the old price. That door closes. So the house is the long-term commitment. The rate is just something you can adjust later.

## When Waiting Actually Makes Sense

I am not going to pretend waiting is always wrong. Sometimes it is the right call.

- Your credit needs work and a few months of effort will really improve your terms.
- Your income is about to change and you want it steady first.
- You have no savings cushion and need a small reserve before you buy.
- The payment simply does not fit your budget yet.

That last one matters most to me. If a comfortable payment is not possible right now, then waiting to strengthen your position is smart. That fits my whole philosophy.

## The Honest Bottom Line

Waiting to buy is not automatically the safe choice. Between rent paid, prices rising, and rates that might climb, the cost of waiting can be very real. Sometimes buying a comfortable home today beats waiting for a perfect day that never quite arrives.
