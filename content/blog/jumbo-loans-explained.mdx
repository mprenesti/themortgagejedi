Some homes are just bigger than a standard loan is built for. Maybe it is a place up in the hills with a view of the Strip. Maybe it is a custom build in Summerlin or a gated estate in Henderson. When the price climbs past a certain line, a regular conforming loan cannot cover it. That is where a jumbo loan comes in.

Let me explain what jumbo really means, and how I help buyers land these homes without overreaching.

## What Counts as a Jumbo Loan

There is a limit on how much you can borrow with a standard conforming loan. It changes over time and by county. Anything above that limit is called a jumbo loan, because the loan amount is bigger than what the usual programs allow.

In and around Las Vegas, plenty of beautiful homes sit above that line. Once you cross it, the rules shift a little. The loan is not backed the same way, so lenders look harder at the details before they say yes.

## How Jumbo Loans Are Different

Because the loan amounts are larger, lenders want more confidence. Here is what that usually means for you:

- A stronger credit profile. Higher scores help a lot.
- A larger down payment in many cases.
- More cash reserves, meaning money left in the bank after closing.
- Cleaner, fuller documentation of your income and assets.

None of that should scare you off. It just means the file has to be tight. When I take on a jumbo client, I make sure we have everything lined up before we go to the lender so there are no surprises.

## The Reserve Requirement Trips People Up

One thing catches folks off guard. On a jumbo loan, lenders often want to see reserves, which is money you keep after the down payment and closing costs. They want proof you could keep paying if life throws a curveball.

That is actually a healthy way to think about a big purchase. If buying the home drains every last dollar, that is a warning sign no matter what the lender says. I would rather you keep a real cushion. A comfortable payment and a healthy savings account go hand in hand.

## My Payment-First Take On Big Homes

Here is my honest view. Just because a lender will approve a huge number does not mean you should borrow it. Big homes come with big everything. Bigger payment, bigger property taxes, bigger insurance, bigger upkeep. The pool, the yard, the square footage. It all costs money every month.

So before we talk price, we talk payment. I want to know:

- What monthly number feels comfortable to you, not just possible.
- What the full cost of ownership looks like, taxes and insurance included.
- How much you keep in reserves after you close.
- Whether this payment still feels good if your income dipped for a few months.

If the answers line up, a jumbo loan can get you into a stunning Las Vegas home. If they do not, I will tell you the truth and we adjust.
