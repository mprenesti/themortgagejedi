A gift of equity lets a family member sell you their home for less than it is worth and have the difference count as your down payment. If a parent, grandparent, or other relative is selling to you, it can be one of the more useful tools in a purchase.

It also has more moving parts than a regular sale. Here is how it works, who qualifies, and what the lender needs.

## What a Gift of Equity Is

Equity is the gap between what a home is worth and what is owed on it. In a gift of equity, the seller gives some of that gap to the buyer instead of collecting it at closing.

HUD defines gifts as [contributions of cash or equity with no expectation of repayment](https://www.hud.gov/sites/dfiles/OCHCO/documents/4000.1hsgh-glossary-011823.pdf). The key words are no repayment. If the family member expects the money back, it is a loan, and that changes the file.

## A Simple Example

Here is a hypothetical example. Say a parent's home appraises at $450,000, and the parent gives you $50,000 of equity.

- That $50,000 works as a down payment of about 11% of the home's value.
- You finance the remaining $400,000.
- At a 6.75% rate, principal and interest on $400,000 is about $2,594 a month, before taxes, insurance, and any mortgage insurance.

These numbers are for illustration, not a quote. How the sale is written up can vary, and I work out the structure with your title company and escrow officer. The settlement statement has to show the gift.

## Who Can Give One

On a conventional loan, the donor rules come from Fannie Mae's [personal gifts guidance](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-4.3-04/personal-gifts), and they apply to gifts of equity too. Acceptable donors include:

- A spouse, child, or other dependent
- Anyone related by blood, marriage, adoption, or legal guardianship
- A domestic partner, a fiancé or fiancée, or someone with a long-standing familial-like relationship

The donor cannot be affiliated with the builder, developer, real estate agent, or another interested party. A seller who meets the donor rules and has no such affiliation can give the gift.

FHA uses its own definition of a family member, which covers parents, grandparents, siblings, spouses, and certain in-laws, among others. Confirm the donor qualifies before the contract is written, not after.

## What Lenders Require

Fannie Mae's guide on [gifts of equity](https://selling-guide.fanniemae.com/sel/b3-4.3-05/gifts-equity) is clear on a few points.

- Gifts of equity are permitted for principal residence and second home purchases.
- The gift can fund all or part of the down payment and closing costs. It cannot be used toward financial reserves.
- The file needs a signed gift letter and the settlement statement listing the gift of equity.

The gift letter has to be signed by the donor and state the dollar amount. It also has to include the donor's statement that no repayment is expected, plus the donor's name, address, phone number, and relationship to you.

## Do You Still Need Your Own Money?

It depends on the property. On a one-unit principal residence with a loan-to-value above 80%, Fannie Mae requires no minimum borrower contribution, and all the funds needed can come from a gift.

For a two- to four-unit principal residence or a second home above 80% loan-to-value, the borrower has to put in 5% from their own funds. Other loan programs set their own rules. My post on [down payment options](/resources/blog/down-payment-options) compares the programs side by side.

## Gift Funds Are a Different Tool

Many people mix these up. Gift funds are cash that moves from the donor's account to yours or to the closing agent. My post on [gift funds for a down payment](/resources/blog/gift-funds-down-payment) covers the paper trail for that.

A gift of equity involves no cash moving to you. The credit shows up on the settlement statement. If a family member wants to give you both equity and cash, tell me early so I can plan the documents for each.

## The Appraisal Matters

The gift is the difference between the appraised value and the price, so the appraisal drives the numbers. A low appraisal can shrink the gift, and a higher one can change your loan-to-value. My post on [the appraisal process](/resources/blog/appraisal-process-explained) explains what to expect.

Talk to me before you and your family member agree on a price. It is much easier to plan around the appraisal than to rework a contract afterward.

## Tax Questions for the Donor

The IRS treats a transfer for less than full value as a gift. In its [Form 709 instructions](https://www.irs.gov/instructions/i709), the annual gift exclusion for 2025 is $19,000, and filers must report gifts above that amount whether or not tax is due.

I am not a tax professional, so this is a conversation for your family member and a CPA. It is better to have it before closing than after. Also ask your title company how the transfer is recorded and whether any transfer taxes apply.

## What Else to Plan For

A gift of equity does not skip the rest of the loan. You still need to qualify on income, credit, and debt, and you still have closing costs. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a gift of equity?

A gift of equity happens when a family member sells you a home for less than it is worth and the difference counts as a gift toward your purchase, with no repayment expected. It can work as all or part of your down payment.

### Who can give a gift of equity on a conventional loan?

Fannie Mae accepts gifts from a spouse, child, or other dependent, or anyone related by blood, marriage, adoption, or legal guardianship. It also accepts a domestic partner, a fiancé or fiancée, and someone with a long-standing familial-like relationship. ### How is a gift of equity different from gift funds?

Gift funds are cash a donor gives you, and the money moves into your account or to the closing agent. A gift of equity is not cash. It is a credit shown on the settlement statement because the seller is selling below the home's value. Both need a signed gift letter.

### Do I still need an appraisal if a family member is selling to me?

In practice, yes. A gift of equity is the gap between the home's value and the price, so the lender needs an appraisal to measure it. Talk to your lender before the contract is written, because the appraised value can change the numbers.

### Can I use a gift of equity on an investment property?

Not with Fannie Mae. Its guide permits gifts of equity for principal residence and second home purchases. Other lenders and programs can differ, so ask before you plan around it.

### Are there tax consequences when a family member gives equity?
