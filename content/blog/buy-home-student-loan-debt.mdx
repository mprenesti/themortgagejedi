I hear it all the time. Someone tells me they want to buy a home, then they lower their voice and say the words like a confession. "But I have student loans." Take a breath. You are not disqualified. Not even close.

Student loan debt is one of the most common things I see with buyers in Las Vegas and Henderson. Nurses, teachers, engineers, business grads. Most of them buy homes just fine. The trick is knowing how lenders treat that debt, and how to set yourself up so your payment stays comfortable.

## How Lenders Actually Look at Student Loans

Lenders care about one number more than almost anything. It is called your debt-to-income ratio, often shortened to DTI. It compares your monthly debts to your monthly income.

Your student loan payment counts as one of those monthly debts. So the size of that payment matters more than the total balance. Someone can owe a big balance but have a small monthly payment and still look great on paper.

Here is where it gets interesting. Different loan programs count student loans in different ways.

- Some programs use the actual payment on your credit report.
- Some use a percentage of the total balance when the payment shows as zero.
- Some let us use your documented income-driven payment.

That flexibility is a big deal. The same buyer can look very different depending on which program we choose.

## Steps to Get Ready

You do not need to pay off every loan before you buy. You just need a smart plan. Here is what I tell people in Nevada to focus on.

- Know your student loan status. Are you in repayment, deferment, or an income-driven plan?
- Pull your current monthly payment amount. Have it in writing.
- Avoid opening new debt right before you apply.
- Make every payment on time. Your history matters more than the balance.
- Keep a little cash reserve so you feel steady, not stretched.

If you are on an income-driven repayment plan, get documentation of that payment. It can lower how much of the debt counts against you, and that helps your DTI.

## The Payment-First Approach

My whole philosophy is simple. I care about the monthly payment you can live with, not the biggest loan you could technically qualify for. With student loans in the mix, that matters even more.

I would rather find you a home with a payment that leaves room to breathe. Room for your loan payment, your life, and a little savings. A house that stresses your budget is not a win, no matter how nice it looks.

So we build the plan around your real numbers. Your income, your student loan payment, and a mortgage payment that fits on top without crushing you.

## A Quick Example

Say two buyers each owe forty thousand in student loans. One has a payment of one hundred dollars a month on an income-driven plan. The other has a payment of four hundred dollars a month. The first buyer will usually qualify for more home, or the same home with more comfort, simply because that monthly number is smaller.

Same debt on paper. Very different outcomes. That is why the details matter and why shopping the right program pays off.
