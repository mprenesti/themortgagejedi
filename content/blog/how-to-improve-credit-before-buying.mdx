Let me start with the part nobody tells you. You might not need to fix your credit at all. A lot of folks in Las Vegas put their home search on hold for a year because they think their score is too low. Then they find out they could have bought months ago.

So before you spend a year stressing over your credit, let's figure out if it even matters for your situation.

## First, Know Where You Actually Stand

Guessing is the enemy here. The score you see on a free app is not always the score a lender pulls. Lenders use a mortgage specific model, and the number can come back different.

The only way to know is to have someone pull your credit the right way. That is a quick, no pressure step, and it tells us three things:

- Your real mortgage score.
- What is helping you.
- What is dragging you down.

Once we see the actual report, we stop guessing. We make a plan based on facts. Sometimes the plan is simple. Sometimes the plan is "you're already good, let's go."

## Do You Even Need a High Score?

Here is the honest truth. You do not need perfect credit to buy a home in Las Vegas or Henderson.

Different loan programs have different minimums. Some allow lower scores than people expect. A score in the 600s can still get you into a home with the right program. And working with multiple wholesale lenders means we can match you to the one that fits your number, instead of forcing your number to fit one bank.

So the real question is not "how do I get to 800." The real question is this. What score do I need for the payment I want? That is a payment-first way to think about it, and it is how I coach every client.

## The Fastest Ways to Move Your Score Up

If we do decide a bump would help your rate or your program, here are the moves that actually work. Not the myths. The real ones.

- Pay down credit card balances. This is the big one. Your balance compared to your limit, called utilization, has a huge effect. Getting cards under 30 percent of the limit helps. Under 10 percent helps even more.
- Do not close old cards. An old card is good history. Closing it can shorten your credit age and shrink your available credit. Both can drop your score.
- Keep making every payment on time. Payment history is the largest piece of your score. One late payment can sting for a while.
- Ask about a rapid rescore. If you pay something down, a lender can sometimes update your score in days instead of waiting a full cycle. This is a tool I use when timing matters.
- Do not open new accounts right now. A new car loan or a new credit card can ding your score and change your debt picture at the worst time.

Small, steady moves beat dramatic ones. You do not need to overhaul your whole financial life. You need to clean up a few specific things.

## What to Leave Alone

Some advice out there does more harm than good. Be careful with these.

- Do not pay off an old collection without asking first. In some cases it can actually reset the date and hurt you. Talk it through before you send money.
- Do not fall for credit repair companies that promise miracles. Most of what they do, you can do yourself for free.
- Do not drain your savings to boost your score. You still need money for the down payment and closing. A slightly higher score with no cash in the bank does not help you buy.
