I have helped a lot of first-time buyers get keys in their hands here in Las Vegas. And over the years I have seen the same handful of mistakes show up again and again. The good news is every one of them is avoidable once you know what to watch for.

None of this is about being smart or not smart. It is about knowing the game before you play it. So let me save you some pain. Here are the five mistakes I see most, and how to steer around them.

## Mistake 1: Shopping for Homes Before Getting Pre-Approved

This is the big one. You find a house you love, you get excited, and then you find out you cannot actually get the loan. That hurts.

Get pre-approved first. Always. A real pre-approval tells you your price range, your likely payment, and any credit items we need to clean up. It also makes sellers take you seriously in a competitive Las Vegas offer.

How to avoid it: talk to a broker before you talk to a listing agent. Get your numbers straight, then go shopping with confidence.

## Mistake 2: Chasing the Biggest Approval Instead of the Right Payment

A lender might approve you for a number that looks exciting. That does not mean you should spend it.

Here is my whole philosophy. The goal is a comfortable monthly payment, not the biggest approval. A payment that feels fine in a good month can feel crushing when the car breaks down or your hours get cut.

How to avoid it: decide what monthly payment feels good to you first. Then we work backward to the home price. Your budget leads, not the bank.

## Mistake 3: Opening New Credit During the Process

You get pre-approved, then you go finance a new car or open a store card for furniture. Now your debt-to-income has changed and the loan is in trouble.

I have watched deals wobble in the final week because someone bought a couch on credit. Please do not be that person.

How to avoid it: from the day you start until the day you close, keep your credit quiet. No new cards, no big purchases, no moving money around without asking me first. When in doubt, send me a text before you swipe.

## Mistake 4: Ignoring the Full Cost of Owning in Las Vegas

The mortgage payment is not the whole story. First-time buyers often forget about property taxes, homeowners insurance, and if it applies, HOA dues. In parts of Henderson and the valley, HOA fees can be a real line item.

How to avoid it: look at your total monthly number, not just principal and interest. When I quote you a payment, I build in the taxes and insurance so there are no surprises. That is the number that matters.

## Mistake 5: Draining Every Dollar to Close

Some buyers scrape together every cent for the down payment and closing costs, then move in with nothing left. Then the AC goes out in a Las Vegas July and there is no cushion.

How to avoid it: keep a reserve. It is often smarter to put a little less down and hold cash for repairs, movers, and life. This ties right back to the payment-first idea. A home should give you peace, not empty your safety net.
