At the median price in Las Vegas right now, owning costs more each month than renting a similar home. That surprises people, so I want to show you the math instead of just telling you.

What you get for the difference is equity and a payment that mostly stays put. What you give up is cash and flexibility. Whether that trade makes sense depends on how long you plan to stay.

## What Renting Costs Right Now

For a three-bedroom home, I am using about $2,200 a month. Rentometer lists a three-bedroom average of $2,241 across property types in Las Vegas, and Homes.com showed a median single-family rent of $2,195 as of July 2026.

Rent is not the whole picture. Utilities, a security deposit, and renters insurance sit on top of it. Nevada also has no statewide rent control, so your renewal can come with an increase.

## What Owning Costs Right Now: A Worked Example

Las Vegas REALTORS reported a September median of $470,000 for existing single-family homes in Southern Nevada. I will use that price with 5% down, a 30-year fixed loan, and a 6.75% rate.

The rate is for illustration only. [Freddie Mac's weekly survey](https://www.freddiemac.com/pmms) has put the 30-year fixed in the mid 6% range, but that survey assumes 20% down and excellent credit, so your rate may differ.

That makes the loan $446,500. Here is the monthly picture.

- Principal and interest: about $2,896
- Property tax: about $235
- Homeowners insurance: about $150
- Mortgage insurance: about $225

That adds up to about $3,506 before maintenance.

A few notes on those numbers. Clark County assesses homes at 35% of taxable value, and commonly cited effective rates land between 0.5% and 0.75% of market value. I used 0.6%. Look up the actual property on the Clark County Assessor's website before you rely on it.

Published insurance averages for Las Vegas run from roughly $1,250 to $2,200 a year depending on the source and coverage level. I used $1,800. A real quote on the actual home beats any average. Mortgage insurance depends on your credit score and down payment.

Then there is maintenance. [Fannie Mae suggests](https://yourhome.fanniemae.com/own/how-build-your-maintenance-and-repair-budget) budgeting 1% to 4% of a home's value per year, with older homes toward the higher end. At 1% on $470,000, that is $4,700 a year, or about $390 a month. Add it in and the total is about $3,900.

HOA dues are not in this example. If the home has one, add it. My post on [Las Vegas HOA fees and your mortgage](/resources/blog/las-vegas-hoa-fees-mortgage) shows how lenders count them.

Against $2,200 in rent, owning runs about $1,300 to $1,700 more per month in this example. A condo or townhome changes the numbers a lot. The September median for those was $290,000, though HOA dues matter more at that price point.

## What Each Path Builds

Owning builds equity through every payment. In year one of this example, about $4,750 goes toward principal. That is money you get back when you sell, less the cost of selling.

Appreciation could add to that, but nobody can promise it. Las Vegas REALTORS reported that the September median matched September 2025, so I would not count on price growth to do the work. Mortgage interest and property tax deductions depend on your situation, so confirm those with a tax professional.

Renting builds flexibility. The $1,300 or more you are not spending each month can go into savings or investments, and you can move when your lease ends. That only works if the difference actually gets saved.

## The Costs People Forget

Buyers forget closing costs, which in my experience run 2% to 5% of the loan amount. They forget maintenance. They forget that insurance, taxes, and HOA dues can change from year to year. My [mortgage budgeting post](/resources/blog/budgeting-for-a-mortgage) walks through every piece.

Renters forget that increases at renewal compound over time, and that a lease gives you no say if the owner decides to sell.

## How Long You Need to Stay

Start with what it costs to get in. In this example, that is the $23,500 down payment plus closing costs. At 3% of the loan, closing costs are about $13,400. That is roughly $37,000 before you pick up the keys.

Getting out costs money too, since selling comes with its own costs. Principal paydown of about $4,750 in year one covers only a small slice of what you put in. That is why a short stay tends to favor renting.

Equity builds faster in later years, because more of each payment goes toward principal. Appreciation could shorten or lengthen the timeline. I will not give you a magic number of years, because it depends on your price, your rate, and your plans.

## Ways to Shrink the Gap

The gap is not fixed. A few things can move it.

Nevada down payment assistance can reduce what you bring to closing. I cover the programs in my [post on down payment assistance in Nevada](/resources/blog/down-payment-assistance-nevada). Seller concessions can cover closing costs, and a temporary rate buydown can lower your payment in the early years.

A lower price point also changes the math. My post on [the income needed for a $500,000 home](/resources/blog/income-needed-500k-home-las-vegas) shows how price translates into the income lenders look for.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is it cheaper to rent or buy in Las Vegas right now?

In my worked example at the $470,000 September median, owning runs about $3,500 to $3,900 a month and renting a similar three-bedroom runs about $2,200. Owning builds equity through principal paydown, and renting keeps your cash flexible. Your down payment, rate, and how long you stay change the answer.

### How much do I need to put down to buy?

FHA loans allow as little as 3.5% down with a credit score of 580 or higher, and some conventional programs allow 3% for qualified first-time buyers. Nevada down payment assistance can reduce what you bring to closing. A larger down payment lowers your payment and can reduce or remove mortgage insurance.

### What costs do buyers forget to budget for?

Closing costs, maintenance, homeowners insurance, property tax, mortgage insurance, and HOA dues. Insurance, taxes, and HOA dues can also change from year to year. Budget for all of them, not just principal and interest.

### How long do I need to stay in a home for buying to make sense?

There is no single number. You put money in up front through the down payment and closing costs, and you pay again when you sell, so equity needs time to catch up. A calculator that uses your real numbers will give you a better answer than a rule of thumb.

### Will my rent keep going up?

Nevada does not have statewide rent control, so your rent can change at renewal. A fixed-rate mortgage keeps your principal and interest payment the same for the life of the loan. Property tax, insurance, and HOA dues can still change.

### Are there programs that lower what I need to bring to closing?

Yes. Nevada Housing Division down payment assistance can cover part of the down payment and closing costs for qualified buyers. Seller concessions and lender credits can also help with closing costs. Eligibility depends on income, credit, and the loan program.
