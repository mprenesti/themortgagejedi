If you spent years in medical school, residency, and training, you know the deal. You come out with a strong future income and a mountain of student debt. A normal mortgage looks at that debt and gets nervous. A doctor loan looks at the whole picture and says, we get it, let's find a way.

I help a lot of physicians, dentists, and other medical professionals in Las Vegas and Henderson buy their first real home. Here is how these loans work and where they fit.

## What Makes a Doctor Loan Different

A doctor loan, sometimes called a physician loan, is built for people in the medical field who have big earning potential but a resume that does not look normal on paper yet. The features usually include:

- Little or no down payment, sometimes zero down.
- Flexible treatment of student loan debt.
- No private mortgage insurance, even with a small down payment.
- The ability to qualify with an employment contract that has not started yet.

That last one is huge. If you signed a contract to start at a hospital or practice in a few weeks, some programs let you use that future income today. A normal loan wants to see paystubs first.

## The Student Loan Advantage

Here is where the biggest difference shows up. On a standard loan, your student debt payment counts against you, and that can sink your approval fast. Doctor loans often treat those balances differently. Some ignore deferred payments. Some use a smaller calculated payment instead of the full number.

For a young physician carrying six figures of student debt, that flexibility can be the whole ballgame. It is the difference between buying now and waiting years.

## Who Qualifies

Programs vary, but these loans usually open up to:

- Medical doctors, MD and DO.
- Dentists and dental specialists.
- Some programs include veterinarians, pharmacists, and other high earning medical roles.
- Residents, fellows, and attending physicians.

Not every lender defines the field the same way. Because I work with multiple wholesale lenders, I can match your specific title and situation to the program that treats you best.

## Zero Down Sounds Great, But Slow Down

I have to be honest with you here, because that is how I do business. Zero down feels amazing, and for the right person it is a smart move. But buying with nothing down means you start with no equity, and your payment will be higher than if you put money in.

Before we chase the zero down option, I want to ask a few questions:

- Do you have a cash cushion left after you buy?
- Is the monthly payment comfortable on your real budget today, not your future budget?
- Are you planning to stay put for a while, or could you move again for work?

The whole point of my payment-first approach is that the home should fit your life, not stress it. A doctor loan gives you options. My job is to help you pick the option that lets you sleep at night.

## Keeping the Payment Comfortable

You have a strong income coming, but training years can be tight. I would rather set you up with a payment that feels easy than max you out on the biggest house you qualify for. We can always upgrade later. What you cannot easily undo is buying more house than your monthly budget wants to carry.

So we run the numbers a few different ways. Different down payments, different price points, different loan structures. Then you get to choose with clear eyes.
