Say the words "reverse mortgage" and people tense up. They picture the bank taking the house, or the kids getting cut out of the inheritance, or some scam aimed at seniors. I get it. There was a lot of bad information floating around for years.

But the reverse mortgage of today is a regulated product with real protections. Used the right way, it can help a Las Vegas homeowner stay put and stay comfortable in retirement. Let me clear up the myths and give it to you straight.

## What a Reverse Mortgage Actually Is

A reverse mortgage lets homeowners age 62 and older turn part of their home equity into cash. Instead of you making a monthly payment to the lender, the lender can pay you. You can take the money as a lump sum, a line of credit, monthly payments, or a mix.

You still own your home. Your name stays on the title. You are still responsible for property taxes, insurance, and keeping the place up. The loan gets paid back later, usually when you sell, move out, or pass away.

That is the core of it. It is a tool to tap the equity you already built without selling and moving.

## The Myths I Hear All The Time

Let me knock down the big ones.

**"The bank takes my house."** No. You keep the title and you keep living there. The lender does not own your home.

**"My kids get nothing."** Your heirs inherit the home. They can sell it, pay off the balance, and keep whatever equity is left. Or they can keep the home by paying off the loan.

**"I could owe more than the house is worth."** These loans are non-recourse. That means neither you nor your heirs ever owe more than the home is worth when it is sold. That protection is built in.

**"It is a scam."** The main program is government insured and comes with required counseling from an independent third party before you can move forward. That step exists to protect you.

## Who It Fits, And Who It Does Not

A reverse mortgage is not for everyone, and I would never pretend it is. It can fit well if:

- You are 62 or older with solid equity in your Las Vegas or Henderson home.
- You want to stay in your home for the long haul.
- You need extra cash flow or a safety net in retirement.
- You are comfortable keeping up with taxes, insurance, and upkeep.

It is a weaker fit if you plan to move soon, or if a family member could cover the same need more simply, or if keeping up with taxes and insurance would be a struggle.
