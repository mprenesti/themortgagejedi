Home prices in Las Vegas have climbed a lot, and for a lot of families a manufactured home is a smart, affordable way to actually own instead of rent. These are not the flimsy trailers people picture from decades ago. Modern manufactured homes are well built, comfortable, and can be a great place to plant roots.

The financing side confuses people, though, and I do not blame them. There are a few different loan types and a few important terms that decide everything. Let me clear it all up so you know exactly what you are dealing with.

## First, Know the Difference in Terms

The words matter here, because lenders treat these homes differently based on how they were built and when.

- A mobile home is technically a factory built home made before 1976
- A manufactured home is a factory built home made in 1976 or later, under updated federal safety standards
- A modular home is built in sections and assembled on site, and it is usually financed much like a regular house

Most lenders will only finance manufactured homes built after 1976. If you are looking at an older mobile home, financing gets much harder, so it helps to know that going in.

## Real Property vs Personal Property

This is the single most important thing to understand, so stick with me.

A manufactured home can be classified two ways:

- Real property, which means the home is permanently attached to land you own and titled together with that land
- Personal property, which means the home is treated more like a vehicle, often in a park where you rent the lot

This one detail changes everything. When the home is real property on land you own, you can qualify for the same kinds of mortgages as a traditional house. When it is personal property, you are usually looking at a chattel loan, which works differently and often carries a higher rate and shorter term.

Whenever possible, I steer clients toward the real property route. Better rates, better terms, and it builds real equity over time.

## The Loan Options You Have

The good news is that manufactured homes on owned land qualify for most of the major loan programs:

- FHA loans, with a low down payment and easier credit standards
- VA loans, for eligible veterans, often with no down payment
- USDA loans, in eligible areas around the Las Vegas valley, sometimes with no down payment
- Conventional loans, for buyers with stronger credit who want to avoid long term mortgage insurance

For homes classified as personal property, chattel loans are the usual path. They can still work, and sometimes they are the only option, but we go in with clear eyes on the rate and the term.

## What Lenders Look At

Qualifying for a manufactured home loan is a lot like any other mortgage. Lenders want to see:

- A credit score that shows you handle your bills
- A down payment, which varies by program
- Steady, documentable income
- The home meets HUD standards and is on a permanent foundation for the best loan types

The home itself also matters. It usually needs to be a permanent structure, properly tied down, with the axles and towing gear removed. These details help the home count as real property.

## The Payment Is Where We Start

Here is my whole philosophy in one sentence. A manufactured home should make your life easier, not harder, and that starts with a payment you can comfortably handle.

A lot of the appeal of a manufactured home is affordability, so it would be a shame to stretch that budget too thin. We start with the monthly payment you feel good about. Then we choose the loan type, the down payment, and the term that get you there.
