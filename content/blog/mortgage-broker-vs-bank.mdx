When people start looking for a home loan in Las Vegas, most walk into their bank first. It feels natural. You already have an account there. You trust the name. I get it.

But here is the thing. Your bank is only one store. When you shop for a loan at one store, you get one set of prices. That is fine for a gallon of milk. For the biggest purchase of your life, you deserve to compare.

Let me break down the real difference between a mortgage broker and a bank, and why it matters for your payment.

## What a Bank Actually Offers

A bank lends its own money using its own rules. That means one menu.

- One set of rates.
- One set of programs.
- One set of guidelines you either fit or you do not.

If your situation is simple and their price happens to be sharp, great. But if you have student loans, self-employment income, or anything a little outside the box, one menu can leave you stuck. And you may never know if a better deal existed somewhere else.

## What a Broker Does Differently

A broker does not lend its own money. A broker shops your loan to many lenders and brings back the best fit. Think of me as your personal loan shopper.

I work with multiple wholesale lenders. Wholesale is the key word. These are rates and programs that are not offered directly to the public. They come through brokers. So the pricing can be better than what you would find walking into a branch.

When your file comes to me, I can look across those lenders and ask a simple question. Who gives this client the best combination of rate, fees, and payment? Then I send your loan there.

## Why This Protects Your Payment

My whole approach is payment-first. I am not chasing the biggest loan you can qualify for. I am chasing the monthly payment you will feel good about for years.

Shopping multiple lenders is how I protect that payment. A small difference in rate can change your monthly cost by a real amount. Over the life of the loan, that adds up to serious money. When I can compare, I can fight for the number that fits your budget.

A bank cannot do that for you. It can only offer its own price and hope you say yes.

## More Programs Means More Solutions

Every buyer is different. A single bank has limited tools. A broker has a full toolbox.

- Buying with student loan debt? Some lenders count that debt more kindly than others.
- Self-employed? Some programs read your income in a smarter way.
- Veteran? I can shop VA loans across lenders for the best terms.
- Investor? I have options like DSCR loans that many banks do not offer at all.

If one lender says no, I do not have to give up. I take your file down the street to the next one. That flexibility is the whole point.

## What About Service?

People sometimes worry that a broker is less personal. In my experience it is the opposite. At a big bank, your file can get passed around like a hot potato. You call and get a different person every time.

With me, you get one person who knows your name, your goals, and your numbers. I am the same face from your first phone call to your closing day, and I am still here after. That is how it should be for a decision this big here in Las Vegas and Henderson.

## A Simple Way to Think About It

Imagine you needed a plane ticket. You could call one airline and take whatever they quote. Or you could use someone who checks every airline and books you the best flight for your needs.

A bank is the one airline. A broker checks the whole board. For your home loan in Nevada, checking the whole board is almost always the smarter move.
