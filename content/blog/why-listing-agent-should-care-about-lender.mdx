If you list homes in Las Vegas, you already know that not every offer is created equal. Two buyers can offer the same price, but one deal closes clean and the other falls apart in the final week. A big part of the difference is the buyer's lender.

I am writing this for the listing agents out there, because the lender behind an offer tells you a lot about whether that offer will actually make it to the closing table. Let me break down why you should care, and what to look for.

## The Lender Can Sink Your Deal

You worked hard to get the listing, stage it, and land an offer. The last thing you want is a lender who cannot perform. When a loan falls through late, you lose time, your seller loses momentum, and the home goes back on the market looking stale.

A weak or overloaded lender can cause:

- Missed contingency and closing dates.
- Surprise conditions that show up at the last minute.
- Appraisal and underwriting delays that blow the timeline.
- A buyer who was never truly qualified in the first place.

You cannot control the buyer's choice of lender completely, but you can absolutely factor it into how you weigh an offer.

## What a Strong Pre-Approval Looks Like

Not all pre-approvals mean the same thing. Some are a quick soft guess. A real one means a broker or lender has already reviewed income, credit, and assets.

When you review an offer, look for these signs of strength:

- The letter references verified income and assets, not just a credit pull.
- The lender is reachable and actually picks up the phone.
- The loan type fits the property and the buyer's situation.
- The lender can speak to the buyer's comfortable payment, not just the max approval.

That last point matters more than people think. A buyer stretched to the top of their approval is a fragile deal. A buyer sitting in a comfortable payment is far more likely to stay calm and close.

## Call the Lender Before You Advise Your Seller

Here is a habit that separates great listing agents from average ones. Before you tell your seller to accept, call the lender on the offer. A two minute conversation tells you a lot.

Ask them:

- Have you verified income and assets?
- What is the buyer's timeline to close?
- Are there any conditions you are still working through?
- How responsive will you be through this deal?

A solid lender welcomes that call. A shaky one dodges it. That signal alone is worth the two minutes.

## Why Working With a Broker Helps Your Listings

When the buyer is working with a broker, you often get more flexibility. A broker is not stuck with one bank's guidelines. I work with multiple wholesale lenders, so if one path hits a snag, I usually have another that keeps the deal alive.

My approach is payment-first. I put buyers into a comfortable monthly payment rather than the biggest approval I can squeeze out. That means the buyers I bring to your listing are steadier and less likely to panic and walk. For a listing agent, that is a smoother path to closing.

## Protect Your Closing and Your Reputation

Every deal that falls apart costs you more than one transaction. It costs your reputation with sellers and your future referrals. Paying attention to the buyer's lender is one of the easiest ways to protect both.

Weigh the lender behind the offer, not just the number on the page. Sometimes the slightly lower offer with a rock solid lender is the better deal for your seller.
