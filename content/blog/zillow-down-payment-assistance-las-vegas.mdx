On October 28, Zillow Home Loans launched a program that covers up to 3.5% of the purchase price on an FHA loan. It is a second loan with no interest and no monthly payment, and it is forgiven after five years if you meet the requirements.

I am a mortgage broker, so I compete with lenders like Zillow Home Loans. I will give you the straight read: this is a real program with real benefits, and it also has trade-offs you should understand before you choose it.

## What Zillow Announced

Here is what [Inman reported](https://www.inman.com/2026/10/28/zillow-down-payment-assistance/) on the program.

- The assistance covers up to 3.5% of the purchase price or appraised value, whichever is lower. That is the FHA minimum down payment.
- It is a second loan at 0% interest with no monthly payment.
- It is forgiven after five years of on-time payments and living in the home. It comes due in full if you sell, move out, or refinance before then.
- You need a 640 credit score and have to qualify for the full monthly payment.
- You must get your FHA mortgage through Zillow Home Loans.
- There is no income cap and no first-time buyer requirement.

The program is available in 43 states and Washington, D.C. [Coverage of the launch](https://www.fox9.com/news/zillow-home-loans-will-cover-your-fha-down-payment-zero-interest-43-states-how-works) lists the excluded states as Arizona, Delaware, Florida, Massachusetts, New York, Rhode Island, and Virginia. Nevada is not on that list, so Las Vegas buyers can use it.

## What It Looks Like on a Las Vegas Home

Las Vegas REALTORS reported a September median of $470,000 for existing single-family homes. Here is what the program would mean on a home at that price.

- Purchase price: $470,000
- Zillow's assistance at 3.5%: $16,450
- FHA first mortgage before mortgage insurance: $453,550

That $16,450 is cash you would not need to bring for the down payment. Because the second loan has no monthly payment, it does not add to what you pay each month. The coverage also says buyers can add their own money, up to a 10% total down payment.

## What It Does Not Do

Here is where I would slow down and read the details.

**It does not remove FHA mortgage insurance.** Under HUD's FHA schedule, the upfront premium is 1.75% of the base loan, which is about $7,937 here and is usually rolled into the loan. A 30-year FHA loan with less than 5% down also carries an annual premium of 0.55% for the life of the loan, about $208 a month in this example.

**It is described as covering the down payment, not closing costs.** In my experience, closing costs run 2% to 5% of the loan amount. On this loan, that is roughly $9,000 to $23,000. My post on [closing costs](/resources/blog/closing-costs-truth) shows where that money goes.

**It does not make the payment cheaper.** You still need to qualify for the full monthly payment on the first mortgage.

**It is tied to one lender.** You cannot take the assistance and shop the first mortgage somewhere else.

## The Five-Year Clock

Forgiveness is not automatic cash. You have to make on-time payments and live in the home for five years. If you sell, move out, or refinance before then, you repay the full $16,450 in this example.

That matters most for refinancing. If rates drop in year two or three and you want to refinance, you would have to repay the assistance first. My post on [whether you should refinance](/resources/blog/should-i-refinance) explains how to run that math, and you can include the repayment in it.

If there is a real chance you will move in the next five years, build that into your decision now.

## The Number to Ask For

The coverage I read describes the first mortgage as carrying a standard rate, and I did not find a published rate for loans that include the assistance. That is not a criticism. It is just the number I would want in writing before deciding.

Here is why it matters. A small rate difference adds up. On a loan of about $461,000, a rate of 7.00% instead of 6.75% costs about $77 more per month, or roughly $4,600 over five years. Those rates are hypothetical examples, not quotes.

So the right question is not only how good the assistance is. It is how good the whole loan is, including rate, points, and APR.

## Comparing It to Nevada Programs

Nevada has its own down payment assistance. Nevada Housing Division programs can come as a grant or a second loan, and some versions never need to be repaid if you meet the occupancy rules. They often have income limits and credit requirements, which the Zillow program does not.

I cover those programs in my post on [down payment assistance in Nevada](/resources/blog/down-payment-assistance-nevada). Confirm current terms with the Nevada Housing Division, since programs change.

If you are not sure FHA is the right loan at all, my post on [FHA vs. conventional loans](/resources/blog/fha-vs-conventional) breaks it down. And my guide to [down payment options](/resources/blog/down-payment-options) covers the lower-down-payment paths that exist outside of any one program.

## Questions to Ask Before You Say Yes

Whichever lender you talk to, get these answered in writing.

1. What is my exact rate, points, and APR with the assistance, and without it?
2. Is the first mortgage priced differently because I am using the assistance?
3. What exactly do I have to do over the five years to have it forgiven?
4. What happens if I sell or refinance before five years?
5. What is my total cash to close, including closing costs and points?

Then put that Loan Estimate next to one from another lender. A comparison on paper is the only way to see which package costs less.

### Is Zillow's down payment assistance a grant?

No. It is a second loan at 0% interest with no monthly payment. It is forgiven after five years of on-time payments on the first mortgage and living in the home. If you sell, move out, or refinance before then, it comes due in full.

### How much does the Zillow program cover?

Up to 3.5% of the purchase price or appraised value, whichever is lower, which is the FHA minimum down payment. Buyers can add their own funds, up to a 10% total down payment. FHA mortgage insurance still applies.

### Is Nevada eligible for the Zillow program?

Yes. The program is available in 43 states and Washington, D.C. Arizona, Delaware, Florida, Massachusetts, New York, Rhode Island, and Virginia are excluded, so Nevada buyers can apply.

### Do I have to use Zillow Home Loans?

Yes. The assistance comes with an FHA mortgage from Zillow Home Loans, so you cannot pair it with a loan from another lender. You also need a 640 credit score and have to qualify for the full monthly payment.

### What if I want to refinance before five years?

The second loan comes due in full if you refinance before the five years are up. A refinance in year two or three would require repaying the assistance, so factor that in if you are counting on a rate drop.

### How does it compare to Nevada down payment assistance?

Nevada Housing Division programs can have income limits and other requirements, while Zillow's program has no income cap and no first-time buyer rule. Terms, rates, and costs differ, so compare a written Loan Estimate for each before you decide.
