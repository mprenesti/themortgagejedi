Getting help with your down payment is nothing to be shy about. A gift from family is one of the most common ways people buy their first home in Las Vegas. It is normal, it is allowed, and it can be the thing that gets you into a house this year instead of three years from now.

But there are rules. And if you do it the wrong way, it can slow down your loan or even cause problems at closing. So let's cover how to do it right from the start.

## What Gift Funds Are

Gift funds are money given to you to use toward your home purchase. The key word is gift. It has to be a true gift, not a loan.

That distinction matters a lot. A loan is debt. Debt affects your approval and your monthly payment picture. A gift is not debt. Nobody expects it back. That is why lenders treat the two so differently.

So the first rule is simple. The money truly has to be yours to keep, with no expectation of paying it back.

## Who Is Allowed to Give a Gift

Not just anyone can hand you gift funds for a mortgage. Lenders want the gift to come from someone with a real relationship to you.

Usually the giver needs to be a family member. That often includes:

- Parents.
- Grandparents.
- Siblings.
- Spouses or partners.
- In some cases, close relatives by marriage.

Certain loan programs also allow gifts from a wider circle, and a few programs are more flexible than others. ## How Much Can You Receive

Here is a question I get all the time. Is there a limit on how much someone can gift me?

For many loan programs, the answer is that a big chunk of your down payment, and sometimes all of it, can come from a gift. The exact rules depend on the loan type and how much you are putting down.

A few things to keep in mind:

- Some programs want you to have a little of your own money in the deal, and some do not.
- The larger the gift, the more careful we need to be with the paper trail.
- The gift can often help with closing costs too, not just the down payment.

The point is, do not let the amount scare you off. A generous gift is a good thing. We just need to document it cleanly.

## How to Document Gift Funds the Right Way

This is where deals get slowed down when people are not careful. Documentation is everything. Do this right and the process is smooth.

Here is what a clean gift usually needs:

- A gift letter. This is a simple signed letter from the giver. It states the amount, confirms it is a gift, and confirms it does not need to be paid back. I will give you the exact template so you do not have to guess.
- Proof the giver had the money. Often a bank statement from the person giving the gift.
- Proof of the transfer. A record showing the money moving from them to you, like a wire or a copy of the check and the deposit.
- A clear paper trail. The amounts should match up cleanly from their account to yours.

And here is the big one. Do not move money around in strange ways. Do not take cash and deposit it. Do not have the gift bounce through three accounts. Underwriters need to trace every dollar. Keep it simple and traceable.

If you are expecting a gift, tell me before it hits your account. That one step saves a ton of headaches. We set it up right the first time instead of untangling it later.
