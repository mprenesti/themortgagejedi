An ADU is an accessory dwelling unit. Out here in Nevada we often call them casitas or guest houses. It is a smaller living space on your property with its own kitchen, bath, and entrance. Some folks build one for an aging parent. Some want a spot for adult kids. Plenty of Las Vegas homeowners build one to rent out and bring in extra income.

The dream is easy. The money part is where people get stuck. So let me lay out how Nevada homeowners are actually paying for these, and how I help them keep the payment comfortable.

## Why Casitas Are So Popular Here

Las Vegas and Henderson lots often have the room for it. Families are looking for ways to house more people under one roof without buying a second home. And with rents where they are, a well built casita can help cover its own cost over time.

A few common reasons my clients build one:

- A private space for a parent or grandparent close by.
- A landing spot for a kid finishing school or saving up.
- A rental unit that adds monthly income.
- A home office or studio that is truly separate from the house.

Whatever the reason, the question is the same. Where does the money come from?

## The Main Ways To Fund an ADU

There is no single "ADU loan" that fits everyone. Instead, we usually pull from a handful of tools. Here are the ones I see work.

**Cash-out refinance.** If you have owned your Las Vegas home for a while and values have climbed, you may have real equity sitting there. A cash-out refinance replaces your current mortgage with a larger one and hands you the difference in cash to build. This makes sense when your current rate is not too far from today's rates.

**Home equity line of credit.** A HELOC lets you borrow against your equity and draw money as the project moves along. That is handy for construction because you pull funds in stages instead of all at once. You pay interest on what you use.

**Home equity loan.** This is a lump sum second loan with a fixed payment. Good if you know your exact budget and want predictability.

**Renovation loans.** Some loan programs let you borrow based on the future value of the property after the ADU is built. That can open the door when you do not have enough equity today but the finished project will be worth much more.

## How Rental Income Changes the Math

Here is where it gets interesting. If you plan to rent the casita, that future income can sometimes help you qualify or help the numbers work. Lenders look at it differently depending on the program. It is not automatic, and I never want you counting on rent that is not guaranteed. But when it is realistic, it can turn a stretch into a smart move.

I always run two versions with my clients. One where the unit sits empty. One where it rents. If the payment is comfortable even when it is empty, now we are talking about a solid plan instead of a gamble.

## Keeping the Payment Comfortable

You already know how I feel about this. The biggest loan you can get is almost never the loan you should get. Before we build anything, I want to see the whole picture:

- What the ADU will realistically cost, with a cushion for surprises.
- What your new monthly payment looks like across each option.
- How it feels in your budget with and without rental income.
- Which choice leaves you room to breathe.

Construction almost always costs more and takes longer than the first guess. So we plan for that up front. A comfortable payment protects you if the timeline slips.
