What if your tenants helped pay your mortgage? That is the idea behind buying a multi-unit property and living in one of the units. Some people call it house hacking. I just call it smart.

You buy a property with two to four units. You live in one. You rent out the others. The rent from your tenants offsets your payment. For the right buyer in Las Vegas, it is one of the best ways to build wealth while keeping your housing costs low.

Let me break down how it works and how to do it the right way.

## The Basic Idea

A multi-unit property has more than one home under one roof or on one lot. A duplex has two units. A triplex has three. A fourplex has four.

When you live in one of those units, the property counts as your primary residence. That is a big deal, because primary residence loans come with better terms than pure investment loans. Lower down payment. Better rates. Easier qualifying.

So you get to live there and invest at the same time. One purchase, two jobs.

## Why the Loan Options Are So Good

This is where living in a unit really pays off. Because it is your home, you get access to owner-occupied loan programs.

- FHA loans can be used on up to four units with a low down payment.
- VA loans let eligible veterans buy up to four units with no down payment.
- Conventional programs also allow multi-unit primary homes.

Compare that to buying a straight rental, which usually needs a bigger down payment and stricter terms. Living in one unit unlocks the friendlier door.

## Rent Can Help You Qualify

Here is a piece a lot of buyers do not know. When you buy a multi-unit property, lenders can often count the expected rent from the other units as income.

That projected rent can boost your qualifying power. So a property that looks out of reach on your salary alone can become very doable once the rental income is added in. This is exactly the kind of situation where the right program makes a huge difference.

## The Payment-First View

My approach is always about the monthly payment you can live with. Multi-unit buying fits that philosophy beautifully.

Think about it. If your total payment is a certain amount, and your tenants cover a big chunk of it through rent, your out-of-pocket cost drops. You might live for far less than you would pay to rent a single apartment in Las Vegas or Henderson.

That is the goal. A comfortable payment, and in this case, help paying it. But I always stress one thing. Run the numbers with real caution. Plan for months when a unit sits empty. A good deal still works even when a unit is vacant for a while.

## What to Watch Out For

House hacking is powerful, but it is not hands-off. Be honest with yourself before you jump in.

- You become a landlord. That means repairs, tenants, and the occasional headache.
- You need a small reserve for vacancies and surprise fixes.
- The units need to be in solid condition to pass loan requirements.
- You have to actually live there. These loans are for owner occupants, not investors pretending to move in.

None of this should scare you off. Just go in with eyes open and a plan. Many great investors in Nevada got their start exactly this way.

## A Simple Example

Say you buy a duplex. Your total mortgage payment is a set amount each month. You live in one side and rent the other side out. That rent might cover half your payment, sometimes more.

Now your real housing cost is far lower than renting or owning a single-family home. Meanwhile you own an appreciating asset and your tenant is helping you pay it down. That is the magic of the strategy.
