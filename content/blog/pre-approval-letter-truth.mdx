You got a pre-approval letter. Congrats. That feels like a green light, right? Here is the part nobody tells you. Not all pre-approval letters are worth the same.

Some are rock solid. Some are barely a guess. In a competitive market like Las Vegas, the difference can decide whether you win the home or lose it. Let me explain what is really going on so you do not get surprised.

## Pre-Qualification Is Not Pre-Approval

First, let us clear up two words that get mixed up all the time.

- A pre-qualification is a quick estimate. You tell a lender your numbers, they do some quick math, and they hand you a letter. Nobody has verified anything.
- A pre-approval means someone actually reviewed your documents. Income, assets, credit, the real stuff.

A lot of buyers think they have a pre-approval when they really have a pre-qualification. Then reality hits during the deal. That is how good buyers lose homes.

## What a Strong Pre-Approval Includes

When I pre-approve someone, I want to look under the hood before you ever make an offer. That means reviewing the real paperwork.

- Recent pay stubs or proof of self-employment income.
- Bank statements and asset accounts.
- A full credit review, not just a soft peek.
- A look at your debts and how they affect your ratios.

When I have all of that, my letter carries weight. A listing agent in Henderson sees it and knows this buyer is real. That confidence can push your offer to the top of the pile.

## Why a Weak Letter Can Cost You

Picture two offers on the same home. Same price. One buyer has a soft online pre-qualification. The other has a fully reviewed pre-approval from a broker the agent trusts.

Which offer wins? Almost every time, the strong letter wins. Sellers do not want a deal that falls apart. A real pre-approval tells them your loan will actually close.

So a weak letter does not just look thin. It can literally cost you the house you love.

## The Payment Behind the Approval Number

Here is where my approach is different. A pre-approval often shows the biggest number you could qualify for. That number can be a trap.

I care about the payment, not the ceiling. Just because you can qualify for a big loan does not mean the payment fits your life. My payment-first approach means we talk about the monthly number you are comfortable with first, then we shop the loan around that.

I would rather you buy a home with a payment that lets you sleep at night. A stretched budget is not a win, even in a house you adore.

## Things That Can Still Change After Pre-Approval

Even a strong pre-approval is not a promise carved in stone. A few things can shake it up before closing, so protect it.

- Do not open new credit cards or finance a car mid-deal.
- Do not make large unexplained deposits into your accounts.
- Do not change or quit your job without telling me first.
- Do not miss payments on anything.

Keep your financial life steady from pre-approval to closing. Boring is beautiful during a home purchase.
