Rates move. Sometimes a little. Sometimes a lot. And every client asks me the same thing. Should I lock now or wait?

It is a fair question. But the honest answer is not "always lock" or "always wait." It depends on where you are in the process and what payment you are trying to hit. Let me walk you through it in plain English.

## What a Rate Lock Actually Is

A rate lock is a promise. The lender agrees to hold your interest rate for a set window of time. That window is usually somewhere from 15 to 60 days.

While you are locked, the rate cannot move up on you. That is the good part. If rates jump next week, you are protected.

But there is a trade. Once you lock, you usually cannot grab a lower rate if the market drops, at least not for free. So a lock is not magic. It is a decision with two sides.

## When a Rate Lock Really Matters

There are moments when locking is the smart, obvious move. Here is when I tell clients to lock.

- You are under contract on a home. Once you have a signed purchase agreement in Las Vegas or Henderson, you have a closing date to protect. Locking removes one big unknown.
- Your payment already works. If the current rate gives you a monthly payment you feel good about, lock it in. Do not gamble a comfortable payment trying to save a few dollars.
- The market feels shaky. When rates are bouncing around and the news is loud, a lock buys you peace of mind. Peace of mind has real value when you are about to buy a home.
- Your closing date is close. If you are 21 to 30 days out, there is not much time to wait around. Lock and move forward.

The theme here is simple. When you have a real timeline and a payment that works, protect it.

## When a Rate Lock Matters Less

Now the other side. Sometimes locking early does not do much for you, or it can even tie your hands.

- You are just starting to look. If you have not found a home yet, there is nothing firm to protect. Your closing date could be months out.
- You are far from closing. A long lock can cost more, and a lot can change before you sign.
- You are watching for a dip and rates are trending down. In that case, we may hold off a bit. But this is a judgment call, not a sure thing.

I never want a client chasing the bottom of the market. Nobody times it perfectly. Not me, not the big banks, not the guy on TV. The goal is a good payment, not a perfect rate.

## The Payment Is the Real Story

Here is where I differ from a lot of folks in this business. I do not obsess over the rate number by itself. I care about your monthly payment and whether it fits your life.

A slightly higher rate with a payment you can live with beats a lower rate you had to stress and scramble for. Because working with multiple wholesale lenders, I can shop your loan and find the combination that lands on a payment that feels right.

Ask yourself these questions instead of staring at rate charts:

- Does this monthly payment fit my budget with room to breathe?
- Am I protected through my closing date?
- Am I making a calm decision or a panicked one?

If the payment works and you are protected, you made a good call. That is the whole game.
