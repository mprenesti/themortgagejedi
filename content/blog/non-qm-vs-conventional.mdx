If a lender has told you that you do not fit, the next question is whether you were looking at the wrong kind of loan.

Non-QM loans exist for that reason. They are not better than conventional loans. They are different, and they solve different problems.

## What Conventional Means

A conventional loan follows the guidelines set by Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac. It is the most common type of mortgage in the country, and it works well for buyers with steady, documentable income, solid credit, and a standard property.

Because the guidelines are standardized, pricing is generally the most competitive. That is why I look at conventional first.

## What Non-QM Means

QM stands for Qualified Mortgage, a category of loans defined by federal rules that set standards for how a lender verifies you can repay. A non-QM loan sits outside that box. It still requires the lender to make a reasonable decision that you can repay the loan. It just uses different ways to measure it.

That flexibility is the whole point.

## Where Non-QM Fits

- Self-employed borrowers whose tax returns understate income, using bank statements or a CPA-prepared profit and loss statement. See [Bank Statement Loans Explained](/resources/blog/bank-statement-loans-explained).
- 1099 earners and contractors
- Investors who qualify on the property's rent instead of personal income. See [DSCR Loans Explained](/resources/blog/dscr-loans-explained).
- Borrowers with significant savings or investments but limited monthly income. See [Asset Depletion Loans Explained](/resources/blog/asset-depletion-loans-explained).
- Borrowers with a recent credit event who are otherwise strong
- Buyers of unique properties that standard guidelines struggle with

## What You Give Up

Flexibility comes with trade-offs. Compared with conventional, non-QM loans often mean:

- A higher interest rate
- A larger down payment or more cash reserves
- Different documentation requirements
- In some programs, a prepayment penalty
- Guidelines that vary a lot from one lender to the next

None of those are dealbreakers when the loan solves a real problem. They just mean the decision should be made with the numbers in front of you.

## How to Decide

1. **Check whether you qualify conventional first.** If you do, it is usually the cheaper route.
2. **If you do not, find out why.** Income documentation, credit, reserves, and property type each point to a different solution.
3. **Compare the actual cost of each path**, including rate, down payment, and any fees, before choosing.
4. **Plan the exit.** Many buyers use a non-QM loan as a bridge and refinance into a conventional loan later when their file changes.
