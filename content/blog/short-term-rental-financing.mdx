Short-term rental investing has real appeal in a tourism-driven market like this one, but financing it correctly, and understanding local regulations, takes more homework than a standard rental purchase.

## Regulations Come Before Financing

Before anything else, confirm current short-term rental regulations for the specific jurisdiction the property sits in. Rules differ meaningfully between the City of Las Vegas, unincorporated Clark County, Henderson, and North Las Vegas, and they have shifted over recent years. Buying a property assuming it can operate as a short-term rental without confirming current permitting rules is one of the costliest mistakes an investor can make in this space. Verify this independently and directly with the relevant jurisdiction before you get attached to a property.

## How Lenders Evaluate Short-Term Rental Income

Standard long-term lease-based rental income calculations do not really apply to short-term rentals, since there is no fixed monthly lease amount. Lenders offering financing for this strategy often rely on platform-based income data, sometimes using tools like AirDNA, to project realistic income based on comparable short-term rental performance in that specific area.

## DSCR Loans Are Often the Best Fit

DSCR loans, which qualify a property based on its income covering its own debt rather than your personal income, are commonly used for short-term rental financing, since they are built for exactly this kind of income-producing property. The property's projected short-term rental income gets compared against its total monthly obligations to determine whether it qualifies.

## Down Payment and Reserve Expectations

Short-term rental financing typically requires a larger down payment than a standard owner-occupied purchase, often in the 20 to 25% range, along with meaningful cash reserves, since these properties carry more income variability than a traditional long-term lease.

## HOA Restrictions Are a Real Risk

A significant number of communities across the valley, particularly newer HOA-governed developments, restrict or outright prohibit short-term rentals. This needs to be verified at the specific property level, not assumed based on the general area. An HOA restriction discovered after closing can turn a planned investment into a property you cannot legally operate the way you intended.

## Insurance Is Different Too

Short-term rental properties generally require a specific type of insurance coverage, different from a standard homeowners or long-term landlord policy. Factor this into your cash flow projections early, since it is often overlooked until after closing.
