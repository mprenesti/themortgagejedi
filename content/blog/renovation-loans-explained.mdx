Some of the best deals in Las Vegas are the homes nobody else wants at first glance. Ugly carpet, a dated kitchen, maybe a bathroom stuck in another decade. Most buyers walk right past them. But those homes often sit in great neighborhoods at a lower price, and they can be turned into something special.

The problem is money. How do you buy a home and pay for the fixes when your cash is already going into the down payment? That is exactly what renovation loans solve. Let me break down the two main options and how they work.

## What a Renovation Loan Does

A renovation loan rolls the cost of the home and the cost of the repairs into one single mortgage. Instead of buying the house and then scrambling to fund a remodel, you borrow for both at the same time.

The loan is based on what the home will be worth after the work is done, not just what it is worth today. That is the magic. You get to borrow against the future, improved value.

The two big players here are the FHA 203(k) and the Fannie Mae HomeStyle. They do similar jobs but fit different buyers.

## The FHA 203(k) Loan

The 203(k) is backed by the FHA, which makes it friendly for buyers who do not have a huge down payment or a perfect credit score.

Key features:

- Low down payment, often around 3.5 percent
- More forgiving credit requirements
- Great for primary homes you plan to live in
- Two versions, a limited option for smaller projects and a standard option for major work

The limited 203(k) is perfect for cosmetic upgrades like paint, flooring, a new kitchen, or updated bathrooms. The standard 203(k) handles bigger jobs like moving walls, structural repairs, or a full gut remodel.

Because it is an FHA loan, you will have mortgage insurance. That is the trade off for the low down payment and easier qualifying. We factor that into the payment so there are no surprises.

## The HomeStyle Renovation Loan

HomeStyle is the conventional cousin. It is backed by Fannie Mae and works well for buyers with stronger credit who want more flexibility.

Key features:

- Down payments that can start around 5 percent for a primary home
- Works for primary homes, second homes, and even investment properties
- No mortgage insurance once you reach 20 percent equity
- Fewer limits on the type of improvements

That flexibility on property type is a big deal. If you are eyeing a rental or a vacation home in the Las Vegas area, HomeStyle can fund the fixes where the 203(k) cannot.

## Which One Fits You

Here is how I usually help buyers choose:

- Choose the 203(k) if you have a smaller down payment or credit that needs a little grace, and the home will be your primary residence
- Choose HomeStyle if you have solid credit, want to avoid long term mortgage insurance, or the property is a second home or rental

Neither one is better across the board. They each shine in different situations. That is the whole reason I look at your full picture before pointing you toward one.

## The Payment Is Still the Point

Renovation loans can be exciting, and that is exactly when I remind people to slow down. It is easy to get carried away adding upgrade after upgrade until the payment balloons.

My philosophy does not change here. We start with the monthly payment you are comfortable with. Then we build the renovation budget to fit inside that number. A dream kitchen is not a dream if the payment keeps you up at night.

So we plan it carefully. We look at the purchase price, the repair budget, and the after improved value, and we make sure the final payment lands in your comfort zone.

## A Few Things to Know Before You Start

Renovation loans have a few extra moving parts compared to a regular purchase:

- You will work with licensed contractors and provide bids for the work
- There is a draw process, so the money is released as the work gets done
- The timeline is a bit longer than a standard closing
- An inspection usually confirms the work before final funds release

None of this is scary. It just needs a lender who knows the process, so the whole thing stays smooth.
